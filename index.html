Everything About Farts

Everyone does it. Nobody looks into it. We looked into it.

99%

of a fart has no smell at all

Nearly all of it is nitrogen, hydrogen and carbon dioxide, and none of those smell of anything. The entire reputation of the fart rests on the last 1% — a tiny trace of sulfur. Your nose picks that up at about one part per billion, which is how a rounding error came to ruin so many lift journeys.

Thirteen times a day is normal. It isn't a problem and it isn't a personal failing — it's the exhaust from a tank of bacteria you carry around and feed several times a day without ever asking it first.

Where it comes from

Air you swallowed

Some of it is just air that went down with your food — from eating fast, chewing gum, or drinking something fizzy. It arrives ready-made and leaves without much fuss. This part doesn't smell.

Bacteria eating your leftovers

The rest is made by bacteria in your gut working through the food you couldn't digest — mostly beans, onions and fibre. They breathe out gas as they go. This is the part that gets interesting.

Why they make a noise

Here's the odd part: the sound isn't the gas. It's skin. It works exactly like blowing a raspberry, or a trumpet player's lips — air gets pushed through a small gap and makes the edges flap. The gas itself is completely silent. It only does the pushing.

So three things decide what it sounds like: how tight the gap is (tight and fast is a squeak, loose and slow is a rumble), how much pressure is behind it, and what you're sitting on. That last one matters far more than people expect — the same fart can be twice as loud on a wooden bench as on a sofa.

You always hear it first

A fart travels at about walking pace. The sound of it travels a hundred times faster. If you're two metres away, the noise reaches you about half a second before anything else does.

That half second is the only warning anybody gets — which is the whole problem with the silent ones. They take the warning away.

Two quick warnings

Don't set one on fire Some of the gas genuinely does burn, and the flame can travel back towards you. People have been hurt doing this. It's the one thing on this whole site we'd ask you not to try.
Farting is normal But sudden changes, blood, real pain, or losing weight without trying are not. That's a doctor thing, not a website thing. Nothing here is medical advice.
