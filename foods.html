Substrate Loading

Forty-two foodstuffs ranked on the Flatulence Index — a composite of volumetric yield, odour intensity, campaign duration and reliability — with onset latencies and the acoustic class each most dependably produces.

Department of Nutritional Pneumatics · Flotzy Institute
Conflicts of interest: the authors like all forty-two of these and intend to keep eating them.

Abstract

The colon does not ferment food. It ferments whatever the small intestine failed to absorb. Any carbohydrate that reaches the large bowel intact becomes bacterial substrate, and every gram of substrate becomes gas. This gives dietary flatology a clean predictive rule: the more indigestible the carbohydrate, the greater the yield — while odour is governed by a wholly separate variable, dietary sulfur. We rank 42 substrates against both axes and find, as expected, that the highest-volume and highest-odour foods are largely disjoint sets.

The leading substrates

Rank 1 · FI 89

Baked Beans Navy

The reference standard, and the reason the entire field exists. Navy beans carry the highest concentration of raffinose and stachyose of any commonly eaten food — α-galactosides that humans cannot cleave, because we do not produce α-galactosidase. Every gram reaches the colon intact and is fermented in full.

Special citation · Sugar-free gum Ranked 7th (FI 80) despite containing almost no food. Sorbitol and xylitol are polyols absorbed slowly and incompletely; the unabsorbed fraction ferments completely. A 2008 case report in the BMJ described a patient who lost 22 kg and suffered chronic diarrhoea from consuming around 20 sticks of sugar-free gum per day. The dose here is small; the eighteen-hour duration is the longest in the table.
Special citation · Eggs Ranked only 28th on the composite (FI 62) but 1st of 42 on odour alone (O = 10.0). Egg protein is exceptionally rich in cysteine and methionine, which sulfate-reducing bacteria convert directly into hydrogen sulfide. Volume is negligible. This is the single clearest demonstration in the dataset that the two axes are independent.

Mechanisms of gas production

Every substrate in Table 1 acts through one of six pathways. Understanding which pathway a food uses predicts not only how much gas it will generate but when, for how long, and whether it will smell.

Pathway 1 · Highest yield

α-Galactoside fermentation

Raffinose, stachyose, verbascose. Found in all legumes. Humans lack α-galactosidase entirely, so 100% of the dose reaches the colon. Products are H₂ and CO₂ — voluminous, and odourless. This is the loud pathway.

Onset 5–7 h · Commercial α-galactosidase drops genuinely work.

Pathway 2 · Longest duration

Polyol malabsorption

Sorbitol, xylitol, mannitol, maltitol. Absorbed by passive diffusion only — slow and incomplete. The unabsorbed fraction ferments over an extended window and also draws water osmotically into the bowel, which is where the sugar-free gum stories come from.

Onset 1–3 h · Duration up to 18 h.

Pathway 3 · The odour pathway

Sulfate reduction

Cysteine, methionine, glucosinolates, allicin. Sulfate-reducing bacteria — chiefly Desulfovibrio — metabolise dietary sulfur to hydrogen sulfide. Volume contribution is near zero. Perceptual contribution is total.

Onset 3–5 h · Detection threshold 0.5 ppb.

Pathway 4 · Bimodal

Lactose maldigestion

Roughly 65% of the world's adults downregulate lactase after weaning. In those individuals, dairy sugar arrives in the colon undigested and ferments rapidly. In the other 35%, dairy does essentially nothing. Highest inter-individual variance in the table.

Onset 0.5–2 h · Fastest of all pathways.

Pathway 5 · Slowest

Fibre & resistant starch

Cellulose, β-glucan and retrograded starch ferment slowly and steadily along the whole length of the colon. Low peak output, very long tail. The characteristic profile is a low background rumble that persists into the following day.

Onset 7–12 h · Duration 10–16 h.

Pathway 6 · Fastest

Aerophagia & carbonation

Swallowed atmospheric air and dissolved CO₂ require no fermentation at all — the gas is pre-assembled and merely transits. Onset can be under twenty minutes. Almost entirely odourless, and the primary source of the nitrogen fraction in Part I, Table 1.

Onset 0.2–0.5 h · Mostly belched rather than passed.

Onset latency and campaign planning

Onset latency is the most operationally useful column in Table 1 and the most widely ignored. A substrate eaten at 13:00 with a six-hour latency does not affect lunch. It affects the 19:00 meeting.

Reduction protocol Soaking legumes and discarding the soak water removes a substantial fraction of the α-galactosides. Commercial α-galactosidase supplements are effective and genuinely evidence-based. Gradual, sustained fibre increase adapts the microbiota over roughly three weeks.
Loading protocol For readers approaching this table from the opposite direction — see Part IV(a), where dietary loading is treated as an input to acoustic output rather than as a problem to be solved. The Institute takes no position on which reading is correct.
