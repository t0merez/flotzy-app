Foods, Ranked

Forty-two foods, sorted by how much trouble they cause. Beans win. Rice loses.

🫘

One rule explains almost all of it

Your gut bacteria don't eat your food. They eat whatever you couldn't digest. So the harder a food is to break down, the more of it reaches the bacteria, and the more gas you get. That's it. That's the whole thing.

Smell is separate. That comes from sulfur — eggs, cabbage, broccoli, garlic. A food can be huge on gas and smell of nothing, or tiny on gas and clear a room.

The worst three

Number 1

Baked Beans

The obvious champion, and it isn't close. Beans contain a sugar humans simply cannot break down — we don't have the right tool for it. So every bit of it arrives at the bacteria untouched, and they have a party.

Kicks in after about 6 hours, lasts 14

Number 2

Brussels Sprouts

The smelliest vegetable there is, and near the top for gas as well. Sprouts are packed with sulfur, which is the stuff that actually smells. The only food on the list that scores high on both counts at once.

lots
Smell

Kicks in after about 5 hours, lasts 10

Number 4

Jerusalem Artichoke

People used to call these fartichokes, which tells you everything. Three quarters of them is a fibre we have absolutely no way of digesting. Produces more gas than anything else here, for longer.

Gas
enormous
Smell
medium

Kicks in after about 7 hours, lasts 16

The sugar-free gum surprise Number 7, despite barely being food. Sugar-free gum uses sweeteners your body absorbs slowly and badly, so most of it goes straight through to the bacteria. It also lasts 18 hours, the longest of anything on the list. There's a famous case of someone chewing about twenty sticks a day and losing 22 kg.
The egg thing Eggs come 28th overall but are number one for smell, by a distance. They barely make any gas at all — they just make the gas you already had considerably worse. This is the clearest proof that the two things really are unrelated.

Why rice is last White rice is one of the only things you absorb so completely that essentially nothing reaches the bacteria. Nothing gets through, so nothing happens. This is why it's in every bland diet ever prescribed — and why, if you have to be in a lift tomorrow morning, it's the answer.

When it hits

The "kicks in" column is the most useful one and everybody ignores it. Beans eaten at lunch don't affect lunch. They affect your evening.

Under an hour

Fizzy drinks and gum

This is just swallowed air passing through. It arrives almost immediately and doesn't smell of anything.

1 to 3 hours

Dairy and sweeteners

Fast, if you're one of the many people who can't handle milk sugar. About two thirds of adults worldwide can't.

3 to 5 hours

Eggs, cabbage, garlic

The smell window. Not much volume — this is when things start to become noticeable rather than audible.

5 to 7 hours

Beans and lentils

The main event. Peak output. A bean lunch lands squarely on your dinner plans.

7 to 12 hours

Fibre and bran

Slow and steady rather than dramatic. This is tomorrow morning's problem, not tonight's.

If you need it to stop

What actually works

Soaking beans and throwing the water away genuinely helps. So do the enzyme drops sold for exactly this. Adding fibre slowly over a few weeks lets your gut adjust.
