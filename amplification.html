Acoustic Amplification
& Resonance Engineering

Eleven techniques for increasing sound pressure level and hardening the attack transient — with measured decibel gains, a stackable live signal chain, and a Helmholtz cavity calculator for those pursuing the matter seriously.

Department of Computational Flatodynamics · Acoustics Group

Abstract

Part IV established how flatus propagates. This report addresses the natural follow-up: how to make it propagate considerably more. We treat the emitter as an acoustic system with three tractable stages — source (the reed valve and the pressure driving it), substrate (the surface it couples into), and environment (the volume it radiates within) — and identify eleven interventions across them. Applied singly, gains range from +2 to +9 dB. Applied together, the naive sum reaches +55 dB, which is nonsense; a diminishing-returns model gives a realistic +23 dB, taking a 62 dB baseline emission to approximately 85 dB. That is city-traffic loud. It is achievable with a bucket.

Why an emission is loud

As established in Part I, the sound is produced by the sphincter operating as a lip-reed valve — the same class of oscillator as a trumpeter's embouchure. Sound pressure level at the listener is therefore set by four things, and only four:

  1. Pressure differential across the valve. Radiated acoustic power scales roughly with the square of the flow velocity, and flow velocity scales with the pressure drop. Doubling the pressure is worth about +6 dB. This is why retention is the single highest-yield source intervention.
  2. Coupling efficiency. A vibrating valve radiating into free air is a poor loudspeaker — most of the energy circulates locally instead of radiating. Coupling it to a resonant volume or a reflective plane transfers far more of it outward.
  3. Boundary reinforcement. A source near a hard surface radiates into half a sphere rather than a full one, doubling intensity for free: +3 dB per boundary. Two walls and a floor gives up to +9 dB for doing nothing but sitting in a corner.
  4. Reverberation. In a room with a long RT60, direct and reflected energy sum at the listener. A tiled bathroom adds 6–8 dB of perceived level over a carpeted bedroom for an identical source.

Note that three of the four have nothing to do with the operator. Most of flatal loudness is interior design.

The decibel scale is logarithmic. +3 dB is a doubling of acoustic power. +10 dB is perceived as roughly twice as loud. The +23 dB achievable with the full stack is therefore somewhere near five times the subjective loudness of the untreated baseline.

The Helmholtz cavity calculator

A rigid vessel with a single opening is a Helmholtz resonator: the air in the neck acts as a mass, the air in the body as a spring, and together they oscillate at one strongly preferred frequency. It is the same physics as blowing across a bottle.

Its resonant frequency is:

f = (c / 2π) · √( A / (V · Leff) )

where c = 343 m/s, A = neck cross-sectional area, V = cavity volume, and Leff = neck length plus an end correction of 0.85·√(A/π), because the air just outside the opening moves with the air inside it.

Tune the cavity so that f lands on or just below the reed fundamental of your intended class — 88 Hz for the Sputterer, 132 Hz for the Trumpet — and the resonator will reinforce it. Tune it far away and the cavity does nothing at all except look strange.

On hardness, as distinct from loudness

Operators frequently request not merely a louder emission but a harder one. These are different acoustic properties and require different interventions.

Loudness is amplitude — how much energy arrives. Hardness is attack transient: how fast the amplitude arrives. A sound reaching full level in 5 ms reads as a crack; the identical sound reaching the identical level over 200 ms reads as a rumble. Perceived hardness is governed almost entirely by the first 20 milliseconds.

Three interventions sharpen the attack specifically:

  1. Sudden aperture release rather than progressive. A valve that yields all at once produces a step-function pressure front; one that opens gradually produces a ramp. This is precisely the distinction between FL-005 The Ripper and FL-010 The Crescendo.
  2. A reflective plane within 10 cm. The reflection arrives inside the fusion window and the ear integrates it into the direct sound as a single, sharper event rather than hearing an echo.
  3. A superimposed percussive source — the skin-on-leather seal release above. A separate broadband click at t = 0 is read by the auditory system as part of the same event and makes the whole thing sound considerably more violent than it is.

Note that all three concern the first fraction of a second. Everything after that is decoration.

Why tiled rooms flatter everything. The first reflection from a hard wall 1.5 m away arrives about 9 ms after the direct sound — inside the ~40 ms fusion window. The ear does not hear an echo. It hears one louder, harder event.

Safety, ethics and the limits of the technique

Do not strain Retention is an acoustic technique, not an athletic one. Forced Valsalva against a closed sphincter raises intra-abdominal and intrathoracic pressure and is a genuinely poor idea — the recognised risks include haemorrhoids, syncope and, in rare documented cases, worse. The +6 dB is not worth a hospital visit.
Do not ignite The hydrogen and methane fractions are flammable and the flame front can propagate back toward the source. This is the only instruction in the entire journal that we repeat on every page.
Confirm the class first Never attempt amplification on a specimen showing a moisture index above 6.0 (FL-009). Amplification techniques increase flow rate and remove restriction, which are the two things you least want in that situation. Abort and relocate.
Consider the audience Every technique here works. That is precisely the difficulty. The Institute observes that the gap between can and should is, in this field, unusually wide, and declines to offer further guidance.
