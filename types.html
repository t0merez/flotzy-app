Original Research · Systematic Flatology

A Typology of Flatus

Fifteen acoustically distinct classes, formally described, named, measured and — for the first time in the literature — made audible.

Department of Systematics · Flotzy Institute
Specimens collected opportunistically. Consent was not obtained; it rarely is.

Abstract

Prior classification of flatus has been informal, regional and unrigorous. We propose the first systematic taxonomy, organised on acoustic morphology rather than on odour, since odour varies independently of every audible parameter (see Part I, § 2). Fifteen classes are described. Each is given a binomial name and a set of measured parameters. Thirteen are illustrated with a real recording, drawn from freely licensed archives and matched to its class by measured acoustics — duration, fundamental frequency, spectral centroid, burst structure and pitch contour — rather than by ear. The remaining two are silent by definition, and are presented without audio for that reason.

Playback notice. Every recording plays through your speakers. Consider headphones if your surroundings include colleagues, a library, or anyone who has already asked you to stop. Press Esc to silence everything at once.

How to read an entry

F₀ — fundamental frequency. The pitch of the reed oscillation, set by aperture tension. Low values rumble; high values squeak.

SPL — sound pressure level. Measured at one metre. For reference: a whisper is 30 dB, normal conversation 60 dB, a vacuum cleaner 75 dB.

Odour index. Volatile sulfur load on a ten-point scale. Independent of loudness. Always independent of loudness.

Deniability. The probability, on a ten-point scale, that the event can be successfully attributed to a chair, a door, a pipe, or a nearby dog.

Moisture risk. The likelihood of an accompanying material component. Values above 6 constitute a tactical emergency and should abort any planned emission immediately.

Waveform. The peak envelope of the actual recording, measured at 150 points.

Measured vs estimated. Duration, F₀ and spectral centroid are measured from the recording. SPL, odour, deniability and moisture cannot be recovered from an uncalibrated archive clip and remain estimates.

Systematic descriptions

Plate I · FL-001

The Sputterer

Flatus staccato

The workhorse of the genus and the most frequently encountered class in the wild. Characterised by an unstable aperture that opens and closes repeatedly under inconsistent pressure, producing a broken, stuttering emission rather than a clean tone. The operator typically believes it has finished on at least two occasions before it actually has. Common after carbonated beverages, where CO₂ arrives in irregular boluses.

Field note: the defining feature is not the sound but the false ending. Do not stand up.

Plate II · FL-002

Silent But Deadly

Silentium letale

The apex predator of the genus. A fully relaxed aperture permits laminar rather than turbulent flow, eliminating reed oscillation entirely — no tissue vibration, no sound. Critically, the slow release is associated with prolonged colonic residence time, which correlates strongly with sulfate-reducing bacterial activity. The acoustic warning system is therefore disabled precisely in the cases where it is most needed. This is the central tragedy of human physiology.

Field note: the audio exemplar is near-inaudible by design. This is not a bug. Raise your volume at your own considerable risk.

No recording Every other class on this page is illustrated with a real recording. This one cannot be. A silent emission produces no acoustic signal to record, so the play control is inert and the waveform is flat. The absence is the specimen.

Plate III · FL-003

The Trumpet

Buccina majestatis

A sustained, tonally stable emission with a clearly defined fundamental and strong harmonic series — the closest the genus comes to music. Requires a rare combination of high reservoir pressure and consistent aperture tension, which is why it cannot be produced on demand and why every attempt to do so in company fails. Historically associated with heraldry, announcements, and the sudden re-evaluation of a friendship.

Field note: harmonically rich enough to identify a pitch. Multiple accounts describe successful performance of the opening interval of Reveille.

Plate IV · FL-004

The Squeaker

Stridor angustus

Produced by a maximally constricted aperture — usually the result of a deliberate containment attempt that has partially failed. High tension raises the reed frequency into the 300–450 Hz band, well above the genus mean, giving the characteristic undignified squeal. The rising pitch contour is diagnostic: as pressure drops, the operator tightens further, and the frequency climbs rather than falls.

Field note: the only class in which increased effort produces a worse outcome. Consider surrender.

Plate V · FL-005

The Ripper

Lacerator textilis

Explosive onset, broadband spectrum, heavy non-linear distortion. Occurs when a high-pressure reservoir meets an aperture that yields suddenly rather than progressively — the valve does not oscillate so much as it is overwhelmed. The perceived "tearing" quality arises from rapid amplitude modulation at 20–30 Hz, near the lower limit of pitch perception, which the auditory system resolves as roughness rather than tone.

Field note: the species name refers to the widespread belief that trousers have been damaged. They have not. Probably.

Plate VI · FL-006

The Machine Gun

Rapidus multiplex

A rapid train of discrete pulses — typically eight to fourteen — at a repetition rate of 5–8 Hz, produced by rhythmic aperture chatter under sustained pressure. Distinguished from the Sputterer (FL-001) by regularity: the Sputterer is chaotic, the Machine Gun is periodic. Frequently terminal, in the sense that once initiated it cannot be halted mid-sequence by any known method.

Field note: commonly reported during the act of sitting down, when posture change modulates abdominal pressure in stages.

Plate VII · FL-007

The Bubbler

Bulla aquatica

The submerged form. Water is roughly 800 times denser than air, so the emission cannot disperse — it collapses into discrete buoyant bubbles that rise at 20–30 cm/s and produce sound not at the aperture but at the surface, on rupture. The acoustic signature is therefore delayed relative to the event by the ascent time, giving the operator approximately 1.5 seconds in which to establish an alibi and fail to use it.

Field note: see Part IV for the full aqueous propagation model, including bubble-radius resonance (Minnaert frequency).

Plate VIII · FL-008

The Ghost (“Crop Duster”)

Spectrum ambulans

A silent emission executed while the operator is in motion, depositing a linear concentration gradient along the path of travel rather than a point source. Because the operator has left the deposition zone before detection thresholds are reached, blame propagates to whoever is stationary and nearby. Ethically indefensible; tactically flawless. The only class defined by operator kinematics rather than by acoustic morphology.

Field note: optimal walking speed is 1.2 m/s. Faster produces turbulent mixing and dilution; slower risks self-contamination.

No recording As with FL-002, there is nothing to capture. The Ghost is defined by operator kinematics and by odour, neither of which a microphone detects. The play control is inert by necessity.

Plate IX · FL-009

The Wet One

Periculum humidum

Characterised by a heavily damped, gurgling timbre indicating the presence of liquid at the aperture. Acoustically, the moisture lowers the resonant frequency and broadens the spectral envelope, producing the unmistakable squelch. This class is the only one in the taxonomy that constitutes a formal warning. The literature is unanimous: cease all activity, locate a facility, and reassess.

Category 1 alert Any specimen exhibiting a moisture index above 6.0 must be treated as a failed containment event in progress. Do not attempt amplification (Part IV(a)). Do not attempt anything.

Plate X · FL-010

The Crescendo

Crescendo terribilis

Begins at low amplitude and low frequency, then rises monotonically in both across two to four seconds. The ascending contour occurs because aperture tension increases faster than reservoir pressure decays — the operator, realising the event is audible, attempts containment mid-emission and inadvertently sweeps the reed frequency upward. A rare example of a control input producing the exact opposite of the intended effect.

Field note: psychologically the most distressing class, as the victim can hear it getting worse and can do nothing.

Plate XI · FL-011

The Sneeze-Fart

Sternuto-flatus

Involuntary co-firing. The sneeze reflex produces a violent, rapid rise in intra-abdominal pressure; where sphincter tone is momentarily insufficient, a brief high-amplitude emission is discharged in near-perfect synchrony with the sneeze. The simultaneity is the defining feature and also the operator's only defence, since the two events partially mask one another. Deniability is consequently higher than the amplitude alone would suggest.

Field note: the same mechanism explains the laugh-fart and the cough-fart, which we treat as subspecies rather than separate classes.

Plate XII · FL-012

The Chair Creak

Ambiguitas sellae

Defined not by its production but by its reception. A mid-frequency emission (180–260 Hz) with a stable envelope and minimal harmonic roughness falls within the spectral neighbourhood of leather, vinyl and cheap office castors. Listeners cannot reliably discriminate. This is the single most valuable class in the taxonomy and the only one that can be strategically induced by choosing the right chair — a matter treated in detail under substrate selection.

Field note: shift your weight immediately afterward. Sell it.

Plate XIII · FL-013

The Marathon

Longitudo aeterna

Defined solely by duration: any single continuous emission exceeding five seconds. Requires an exceptionally large reservoir and a stable, partially open aperture that neither closes nor fully releases. Timbre drifts across the event as pressure falls and tension compensates. Bystanders characteristically pass through three phases — amusement, disbelief, and concern — at approximately two-second intervals.

Field note: the exemplar runs 6.5 s. Documented claims beyond two minutes exist but originate entirely from the internet and are treated as folklore.

Plate XIV · FL-014

The Pop

Punctum breve

The minimum viable event: a single impulse of 100–200 ms with almost no sustained oscillation. Essentially one cycle of the reed valve opening and shutting. Because it is over before the auditory system has finished localising it, witnesses are frequently uncertain that it happened at all — a state the operator should not attempt to resolve for them.

Field note: often the audible portion of a much larger silent emission. The Pop is the doorbell; FL-002 is what is standing outside.

Plate XV · FL-015

The Afterburner

Ignis posterior

The upper bound of the genus. Maximum reservoir pressure, maximum flow rate, aperture driven so far into non-linearity that the spectrum approaches broadband noise. Typically follows a legume-dominant loading protocol (see Part III) combined with prolonged retention. Reported SPL figures approach those of a passing motorcycle. Structural resonance of nearby furniture has been described, though never under controlled conditions.

Do not ignite The hydrogen and methane fractions are flammable and the flame front can propagate toward the source. The Institute has received exactly one report on this subject and has no wish to receive a second.

Principal finding Across all fifteen classes, odour index and sound pressure level are inversely correlated (r ≈ −0.62). The quietest specimens are the most dangerous. Loudness is, in effect, an early warning system that fires reliably in exactly the cases where no warning is needed.

Provenance, method and licences

Every audible specimen on this site is a real recording. Nothing is synthesised. Candidate audio was gathered from Wikimedia Commons — including a 4 min 50 s archive compilation released by the Finnish public broadcaster YLE, which we segmented into 62 discrete events by energy gating.

Each of the 83 resulting candidates was then measured for duration, fundamental frequency (autocorrelation with interior peak-picking after a 1.2 kHz low-pass), spectral centroid, spectral flatness, burst count and regularity, attack time, crest factor and pitch contour.

Classes were assigned by scoring every candidate against the criteria in the dichotomous key and solving a global one-to-one assignment, so the taxonomy did the classifying rather than our ears. Thirteen of fifteen classes found a credible match.

Two did not, and are presented without audio: FL-002 Silent But Deadly and FL-008 The Ghost. No recording of a silent fart can exist. That is, after all, the entire point of them.
